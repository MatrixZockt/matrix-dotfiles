Network & internet — four directions

Same data, same actions, four different shapes. Every mockup is driven by this machine's real state right now: ethernet on 192.0.2.10 and Wi-Fi on 192.0.2.11 simultaneously, bluetooth on with nothing paired. Nothing here is invented except the two sample networks in the lists.

What is actually wrong with the page today

  • Ethernet is never mentioned. The machine routes over the wire and the page has no wired section at all — the one thing you most want to confirm is the one thing it cannot tell you.
  • "Scan for devices" is a switch. Scanning is an action, not a state you hold on or off, and as a 190px-wide toggle next to its own label it is the squashed thing in the screenshot.
  • Every label appears twice. The section header says Bluetooth and the row directly beneath it says Bluetooth again, with the same glyph. Same for Wi-Fi.
  • The Wi-Fi tab is a void. With no networks in range the tab is a header, an empty list and 500px of nothing, with no empty state and no action offered.
  • Tabs hide state. You cannot see that the wire is up while you are looking at bluetooth — the page is per-radio, but the machine's connectivity is not.
  • 600px of the width is unused. Everything is pinned to a ~600px column on the left of a 1200px page.
  • The "Network" chip beside the title duplicates the rail item you just clicked.
Direction A

Status rail on top, tabs underneath

The smallest honest fix. A persistent live status strip sits above the tabs and always names every link the machine currently has — wired, wireless, whatever — no matter which tab you are on. Then the existing tab structure, but with the doubled labels collapsed into one header row and the scan switch replaced by a scan button.

Trade-off: keeps the tab you have, so the per-radio mental model survives. The status strip is a new thing to look at, and on a machine with one link it is one row of mostly-empty.

Direction B

No tabs — every radio as its own card

Drop the tab switch entirely. Ethernet, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth each become a card: a header with the toggle, and a body carrying either that link's live details or the list of things you can join. Cards the machine has nothing to say about collapse to a single row.

Trade-off: nothing is hidden, which is the whole point — but the page gets longer, and two radios competing for the same scroll is worse than one when you only came to change one of them.

Direction C

Master / detail across the full width

A settings app is not a document — it should use the width it has. The left column is a short list of radios, each already showing its live state; the right pane is the detail for whichever one is selected, with the connection's facts as a row of figures above the list of things to join.

Trade-off: fills the page and scales to a fourth or fifth radio later, but it is the biggest change and two-pane layouts are awkward on a narrow window.

Direction D

Dense rows, no chrome

The opposite bet from the other three: no cards, no section headers with icons, no figures row. Everything is a row, grouped under three small caps labels — Connected, Networks, Devices — with the status pushed to the right edge. Roughly twice the information in the same height, for people who read by scanning rather than by looking.

Trade-off: the most scannable and by far the least "settings app"-looking. The IP and the signal stop being landmarks because nothing is a landmark — which is the point, and also the risk.
